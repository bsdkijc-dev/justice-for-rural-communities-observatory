# Methodological Limitations & Uncertainties

1. **Iceberg Effect in Court Data:** Published decisions in [Direktori Putusan Mahkamah Agung](https://putusan3.mahkamahagung.go.id) represent only disputes that reached formal adjudication and were uploaded. They do not measure the prevalence of justiciable problems in rural communities, nor do they capture informal resolution or abandoned claims.
2. **Judgment vs. Enforcement Gap:** A court ruling does not prove actual execution, asset recovery, or household economic improvement.
3. **Publication & Digitization Bias:** Upload rates to Direktori Putusan and completeness of village-level statistics vary across regions, especially in 3T (*Tertinggal, Terdepan, Terluar*) and archipelagic districts.
4. **Travel Distance vs. Real Friction:** Straight-line distances severely underestimate travel time and cost in mountainous, riverine, and island geographies where seasonal weather and ferry schedules dominate.
5. **Survey & Qualitative Gates:** Until ethics approval and field protocols are cleared, no empirical household experience data (Dataset 08) exists; synthetic records (`P5`) are strictly for schema and pipeline testing.
6. **Rural Classification Lag:** BPS urban/rural classifications ([Perka BPS No. 37/2010](https://ppid.bps.go.id/upload/doc/Perka_BPS_No_37_Tahun_2010___Klasifikasi_Perkotaan_dan_Perdesaan_Di_Indonesia_Buku_2_-_Jawa_1659514400.pdf)) and periodic PODES cycles may lag rapid peri-urbanization or village *pemekaran* (administrative splits).
